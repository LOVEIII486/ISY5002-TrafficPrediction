\subsection{Human-Labeled LTA Evaluation}\label{sec:results-human-lta}

The LTA results in Section~\ref{sec:results-lta} use the frozen YOLO26x teacher
as reference. The human-labeled set described in Section~\ref{sec:setup-data}
provides an independent accuracy measurement with the same four-class evaluator
at the 1280-pixel batch-inference resolution. It contains 63 week-2 images from
the eight cameras (36 day, 27 night) with 1,197 vehicle boxes: 611 cars,
459 motorcycles, 97 trucks and 30 buses. The annotator started from the teacher
boxes, retained 96\% of them and added 566, of which 385 were drawn by hand,
mostly distant motorcycles. The set was frozen with image-list and label
digests before any model was scored on it.

Table~\ref{tab:lta-human} reports the three students and the teacher on this
set.

On human labels the joint model reaches 0.478 mAP@0.5, against 0.467 for the
pretrained detector. The paired difference of 0.011 (95\% CI −0.043 to
0.060) does not establish an improvement; the two models are also close in the
teacher-reference comparison at 1280 pixels (Table~\ref{tab:lta}). Absolute
scores are lower than against the teacher (0.478 versus 0.638 for the joint
model), because the human labels add many small vehicles that the teacher
missed. The joint model's advantage is coverage rather than overall precision.
With the class-agnostic matching of Table~\ref{tab:filter}, its recall is 0.547
against 0.394 for the pretrained detector, at a precision of 0.816 against
0.872. Its counting error falls from 10.46 to 6.92 vehicles per image, and its
motorcycle AP rises from 0.197 to 0.300. The pretrained detector remains better
on buses and on mAP@0.5:0.95. MIO-only training stays far behind at 0.198,
confirming the domain gap seen in Section~\ref{sec:results-lta}. All detectors
miss many vehicles: the joint model's class-agnostic recall is 0.622 by day and
0.391 at night, and most misses are distant motorcycles.

Three properties of the set limit these figures. The teacher pre-labels favour
the teacher and the students distilled from it. The 17 removed images are the
densest and darkest frames, mostly from the causeway camera 2702 and at night,
so the scores likely overstate performance in those conditions. The set has a
single annotator, and the image-level intervals ignore correlation within the
eight cameras, so they are optimistic.
